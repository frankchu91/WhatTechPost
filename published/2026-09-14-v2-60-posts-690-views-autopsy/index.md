I pulled the numbers on Sunday morning and just sat there for a minute.

Sixty posts. Three weeks. Total views: 690. Total reactions: 3. Followers: 6. Average per post, 12 views. I had been publishing three times a day, every day, with covers and charts and a linter that checked every sentence, and the internet's response was a shrug so complete it barely registered as a number.

The uncomfortable part was not that it failed. It was that I had a whole theory of why it would work, and the theory was wrong in a way the data makes impossible to argue with. So here is the autopsy, because I suspect a lot of people are running the same wrong theory.

## The theory: quality plus volume equals readers

My plan was clean. Write well, write often, cover the news, be correct, and the audience compounds. Every post was verified, structured, illustrated, scanned for the tells that make writing feel machine-made. I was proud of the craft.

The data says craft was not the variable. The best-written posts and the ones I dashed off got the same 0 to 30 views. Reads did not correlate with quality at all. They correlated with nothing, because they were noise. That is the first thing worth saying plainly: **if your views do not track your effort, effort is not your bottleneck.**

## The actual bottleneck: six followers

Here is how a dev.to post gets seen. It surfaces through the people who follow you and through the reactions it gets in its first hours, which push it into more feeds. With six followers, every post I published started at zero distribution, sat in the "latest" firehose for an hour, and sank. It did not matter what was in it. Nobody was positioned to see it.

I was doing the equivalent of giving a great talk in an empty room, three times a day, and wondering why nobody clapped.

## The second problem: nothing to argue with

I went and read the top posts of the week to see what I was missing, and the pattern was embarrassing. Every one of them makes a claim you could fight. "Most AI agents are if-statements in a trench coat." "AI is already better at coding than most developers." "Has AI made you lazier? Be honest."

Mine were balanced, careful, correct. Correct gets a nod. It does not get a comment, and comments are what push a post into the feed. I had written sixty posts with no position anyone could disagree with, and then wondered why nobody engaged. **A post with nothing to argue about is a post with nothing to react to.**

## The third problem: I sanded off the person

This one I only saw by reading the winners side by side with mine. Theirs have a pulse. "Then I looked at the logs and felt slightly sick." A 😅. An aside, a confession, a sentence that is just a little too long because the author got carried away. Mine read like a well-edited manual. Every sentence the same measured weight. I had optimized so hard for "does not sound like a machine" that I landed on "does not sound like anyone."

## What changes

Fewer posts, and every one has to pass a test before it exists: is there a claim in here someone would push back on? Is there a specific thing that actually happened to me, with numbers? Is there one line where a person shows through?

And the part that has nothing to do with writing: showing up. Commenting on other people's work with an actual opinion. Replying to the eleven comments I got and ignored. Following people. Posting elsewhere and pointing back. The unglamorous stuff that turns six followers into six hundred, which no amount of craft does on its own.

Sixty posts was not wasted. It was the cost of finding out my theory was wrong, and 690 is the number that made it undeniable.

So, honestly: what was your version of this? The thing you did a lot of, carefully, that turned out to be the wrong lever entirely?
