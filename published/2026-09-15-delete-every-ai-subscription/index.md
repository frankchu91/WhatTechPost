I ran this on a Sunday because a renewal email annoyed me. Cancel every AI subscription and API key tomorrow. All of them. What actually stops working on Monday?

I expected to feel some panic. Instead I got an uncomfortably short list.

The coding agent, yes, within an hour. Two API-backed jobs that run on a schedule, and I would miss those by lunch. That is the honest list. Everything else, the second chat subscription, the writing tool, the image thing, the two trials that quietly converted, could vanish and I am not confident I would notice for a week.

Which is strange to discover about money that leaves your account every month. The stuff I would actually miss is roughly a third of what I pay for. The rest is habit, mild fear of missing out, and a vague sense that a serious person in 2026 has these tools open.

Here is the part I keep turning over, though, because the experiment is not as clean as it first felt. "Would I miss it" and "is it worth it" are different questions. Some of the tools I would not miss are doing quiet work I have stopped noticing, the way you stop noticing good shoes. And some of the ones I would miss loudly are things I have simply become dependent on, which is not the same as them being valuable. The exercise sorts by attachment, not by value, and I am not sure which of those I should be optimizing for.

It was still clarifying. The gap between "everything I pay for" and "everything I would fight to keep" was much wider than I would have guessed, and I had never once looked at it directly.

So, honestly:

1. If it all got cancelled tomorrow, what would you actually miss by Monday afternoon? The real list, not the one you would defend in a budget meeting.
2. Is there a tool on your bill you have not opened in a month, and what is the actual reason it is still there?

I suspect most of us have never run this, and that the answers are going to be embarrassingly similar.
